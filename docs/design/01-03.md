## 1. Delivery semantics, hop by hop

Each hop may deliver twice; the next hop makes the second delivery a no-op.

| Hop | Semantics | Mechanism and key | Written when, relative to the effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| gateway → reader | effectively-once processing | per-channel `seq`: `seq < expected` dropped whatever `poss_dup` says; `seq > expected` → one `RESEND`, re-asked after 2 s (`reader/domain/sequencer.py`, D13). Resume = `channel_progress.last_seq` on **every** logon (`reader/app.py` `_load_resume`, D14) | resume point is the consumer-written checkpoint, i.e. only what is already **applied** in Postgres |
| reader → broker | at-least-once | `acks=all`, idempotent producer (`redpanda_producer.py:20–21`); key = merchant (D1); `session_epoch` on every message (D16); delivery error → session-fatal, producer recreated (D15, D19) | credit re-granted only **after the broker ack** (`sequencer.py:165` `on_acked` → `:194–196`) |
| broker → consumer | at-least-once | manual commit, `(partition, offset)` | offset committed **after the Postgres `COMMIT` returns** (`consumer/app.py:79` after `:92`) |
| consumer → Postgres | **exactly-once effect** | one txn per poll batch per partition (D4): `processed_events` PK insert `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` (`pg_ledger_store.py:146`); contiguity CAS `UPDATE channel_progress … WHERE last_seq = orig` (`:270–279`); stale epoch skipped (`:169`, D21); out-of-order skipped, dedupe row removed (D18, D22) | dedupe row, ledger effect, `ledger_history` row and checkpoint **in the same transaction** |
| Postgres → executor → bank | exactly-once money movement | lease via `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`; CAS writes on `(status, attempt, lease_owner)`; `Idempotency-Key = "{payout_id}#a{attempt}"`, same key while fresh, search before a new key (D24, D25) | intent (`in_flight`, key, `pending_send_at`) committed **before the POST** (`executor/app.py:209–221`); outcome after the reply |

```
gateway ─seq─▶ reader ─acks=all─▶ broker ─offset after COMMIT─▶ consumer ─PK+CAS, one txn─▶ Postgres ─intent before POST─▶ bank
```

**A delivered, credited frame is never lost (§2 Availability).** `last_seq` only moves in the consumer's apply transaction. A frame received but not yet acked when the reader dies is above `last_seq`; the next session logs on with that resume map and the gateway resends it (72 h history). A frame acked but not applied is in the topic (`acks=all`). A missing frame stalls its channel through the contiguity CAS; it is never skipped.

The balance is deducted **once**, by the consumer, in the transaction that inserts the `payouts` row; the executor never touches balances.

## 2. Fencing: the leader stalls for 45 s

Measured (`scripts/faults.sh`, PLAN.md Step 2): `kill -9` → new session in **8.6 s**; `SIGSTOP` 45 s → standby took over in **9.0 s**, old leader stepped down **0.2 s** after waking. Inside both 15 s (A1) and 30 s (§2). Bound = lease TTL 10 s + standby poll 1 s + logon.

### 2a. Step by step, and what stops corruption at which hop

| t | Old leader L (epoch E) | Standby S | Stopped at hop |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | frozen, holding unpublished frames and a producer buffer | polls the lease every 1 s | — |
| 5 s | silent | — | **gateway → reader:** gateway drops L at 2.5 × heartbeat (`gateway.py:286`) |
| 10 s | lease expires (DB clock, `pg_lock.py:12–17`) | acquires; `LOGON` → **E+1**; `fence.claim(E+1)` | **gateway:** a newer logon supersedes any older session (`gateway.py:380–385`) |
| 10–45 s | — | resumes from `channel_progress`, republishes un-applied frames as E+1 | — |
| 45 s | wakes: socket EOF; `renew` → `LOST` (`reader/app.py:258–266`); lease re-checked before any reconnect (`app.py:113–126`) | leading | **reader:** L can't reconnect over S |
| 45 s+ | producer may flush its t=0 buffer, stamped **E** | — | **broker → consumer:** `E < fence` → `stale_epoch` skip (D21). Even unfenced: each event is a PK duplicate or fails the contiguity CAS |

### 2b. Fencing token and who checks it

Token: the gateway's `session_epoch`, strictly increasing per logon (`gateway.py:381`).

| Checker | Check |
|---|---|
| gateway | newer `LOGON` closes the older session |
| reader on logon | `UPDATE session_lease … WHERE epoch < :e` (`pg_fence.py:6–9`); 0 rows → step down |
| consumer, every batch | `msg.session_epoch < session_lease.epoch` → skip (`pg_ledger_store.py:169`, fence cached ≤ 2 s) |
| checkpoint row | `channel_progress` moves only if `epoch <= msg.epoch` (`pg_checkpoint.py:21`) |

### 2c. Why a TTL lock is not sufficient on its own

A paused process can't see its lease expire; on waking it acts on stale belief. So the lock decides only who *tries* to log on (D12); safety comes from the downstream checks in 2b. Two real failures:
- **Incumbent** (REVIEW L1/L2): renewal was an unconditional `pexpire` (`incumbent/leader.py:34–37`) and nothing carried an epoch, so a woken leader extended the new leader's lock and both "logged session up".
- **Ours, found by fault testing:** the lease holder was `hostname:pid`. A restarted container got the same hostname and PID, re-entered the dead process's still-valid lease, and the standby never saw it free. Fixed with a per-process nonce (`common/config.py:76`).

## 3. Backpressure: 30 k/s in, broker at 10 k/s for 10 minutes

Deficit: (30,000 − 10,000) × 600 s = **12 M frames**.

### 3a. Where the backlog lives, and what bounds memory

**At the gateway**, because credit is granted only from broker acks: delivery raises `unacked`, only the ack callback lowers it (`sequencer.py:165–173`), and `tick` grants only while `unacked < 2,000` per channel and `< 50,000` in total (`:194–196`). A slow broker stops credit, and the 12 M frames wait in the gateway's 72 h history. A channel with an open gap still gets 64 credits so its resend can arrive (D17).

| Process | Bound |
|---|---|
| reader | 50,000 unacked frames ≈ 25 MB (~0.5 KB each, estimate), plus granted-but-unused credit ≤ 1,000 per channel. At 200 channels that's +0.2 M; at 60 k channels it would be 60 M, so `high_water` must come from a global credit budget split by channel rate. **The one bound that does not scale as configured.** |
| reader's producer queue | `BufferError` → poll and retry ≤ 100 × 10 ms (`redpanda_producer.py:30–40`), then session-fatal: the reader slows, never grows |
| consumer | one poll batch: ≤ 200 records per partition (`consumer/app.py:24`) |
| broker | holds only what it accepted; sized for consumer outages: 10 min × 30 k/s = 18 M msgs ≈ 9 GB, × RF 3 ≈ 27 GB |

**Catch-up:** with 2 sessions (2 × 25 k/s, §4) and the broker back above 50 k/s, the surplus over 30 k/s arrivals is 20 k/s → **12 M / 20 k/s ≈ 10 min**. That rises to ~13 min with our measured 24.6 % go-back-N waste (STEP2 notes). Consumers aren't the limit: 64 partitions × ~1,700 ev/s (STEP3 notes) ≈ 108 k/s.

### 3b. How credit participates

Credit follows broker durability, not receipt. So "the broker is slow" becomes "the gateway sends less", and the backlog sits in the one component built to hold 72 h of it.
